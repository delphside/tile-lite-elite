# Seats and invitations

How a game's seats are filled, and how its creator manages them. The calls,
with their requests, responses and refusals, are in
[4.4.3](4.4.3-api-schema.md#seats--roster-endpoints-pre-start-only-except-force-resign-and-abort);
worked examples in [4.2.6](4.2.6-authentication-examples.md); who a player is
in [4.2.5](4.2.5-authentication.md).

## The four ways to claim a seat

Every human seat carries a claim when it is created. An engine seat has none:
its engine fills it at once. The four claims:

| claim | the seat is filled by | notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Creator` | the player creating the game, at once | at most one per request; refused when a seat is added later |
| `Named` | that registered player, when they accept | the name must exist, so a typo fails the whole request rather than leaving a half-built game |
| `Open` | any signed-in player, first to accept | listed in every signed-in player's `GET /games`, tagged `InvitedOpen` |
| `Email` | whoever holds the emailed link and confirms | no account need exist yet; not listed anywhere else |

`Open` and `Email` seats have no proof that the person accepting is the one
intended: `Open` intends no one, and for `Email` holding the link is the test.

## Invitations

A human seat other than the creator's has an invitation, whose status the
seat shows (`ParticipantDto.invitation_status`) while the game is waiting:

| status | means |
| --- | --- |
| `not_sent` | the seat was added after creation and its invitation has not been sent |
| `pending` | sent, not yet answered |
| `rejected` | declined, or accepted and then withdrawn |

A claimed seat, and an engine seat, have no status. An invitation itself can
also end `accepted`, or `cancelled` when its seat is removed. Each seat keeps
its invitations as rows in `game_invitations`: sending and resending add rows.

Where a player sees an invitation:

| claim | seen in |
| --- | --- |
| `Named` | their own invitation list, and `GET /games` tagged `InvitedByName` |
| `Open` | `GET /games`, tagged `InvitedOpen` |
| `Email` | its link only |

**Accepting** binds the player to the seat and replaces the seat's placeholder
name with theirs. Only the named player may accept a `Named` invitation. For
`Open` and `Email`, the first acceptance wins and a second is refused. Only a
`Named` invitation can be rejected; for the others, not accepting is the same
thing.

## Managing the roster

While a game is waiting to start, its creator may:

- **add a seat**, without sending its invitation, so several can be staged
  before any goes out;
- **send or resend an invitation** to an unclaimed human seat, once its previous
  invitation has been answered;
- **remove a seat**, claimed or not, cancelling a pending invitation on it. The
  seats after it renumber to stay contiguous. The creator's own seat cannot be
  removed;
- **swap two seats' turn order**, once every human seat is claimed;
- **start the game**, once every human seat is claimed and there are at least
  two seats;
- **abort the game**, which also works once it is running: it ends with no
  winner and no rating change.

The player holding a seat, other than the creator, may **withdraw** from it.
The seat stays, unclaimed, and its invitation becomes `rejected`, so the
creator can resend or remove it.

Once the game is running, the creator may **force-resign** another player's
seat (not their own), for someone who has stopped responding. The seat drops
out and the others play on; the game ends when at most one seat is left
playing. The move is recorded as `force_resign`, and the seat is left out of the result, unranked
and unrated ([RATE-3](4.1.0-rules.md#ratings)). Resigning and force-resigning
do not need it to be that seat's turn.

Only the creator manages the game. If the creator's account is deleted, the
game has no creator: any holder of a claimed seat may manage it, and in a game
with no claimed seat anyone may, signed in or not
(`caller_may_manage_game`).

## Joining by email

The emailed link opens `/invite?id=<invitation_id>` in the web client:

1. The page reads the invitation's preview, which needs no sign-in: who sent it
   and whether it is still pending.
2. A visitor without a session registers or signs in, with a banner naming the
   inviter. A visitor already signed in skips this.
3. The page asks for confirmation before joining, naming the inviter, so a
   visitor signed in as the wrong player can back out. Confirming accepts the
   invitation.
